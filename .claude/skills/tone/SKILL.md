# /tone

Database skill: the knowledge lives in `references/tone.md`. Keep this file short.
The references start empty and are built by learning, not by a one-off sample dump.

1. Read `references/tone.md` (principles first, then examples).
2. Draft in that voice. When you pitch options (titles, hooks, openers), give 10: 8 follow the references, 2 are wildcards (see `/wildcard`). Label the wildcards. While the references are thin, say so in one line.
3. Learn from every round:
   - When Issam edits a draft, compare the two versions and name the principle behind the change.
   - When Issam gives feedback or picks one option over others, do the same.
   - When Issam sends something they wrote and are happy with, offer to save it as an example.
4. Propose each lesson as a one-line principle plus the example it came from, and save it to `references/tone.md` only after a yes. Prefer one principle that explains several edits over a new narrow rule; merge or rewrite principles rather than stacking them.
5. Log each change in the "Changelog" section of `references/tone.md`: date, what changed, why.
